Overview

Some things still mean more handwritten.

Community Message Courier carries notes, drawings, and voice postcards between neighbors in assisted living, so connection doesn't depend on who has the energy to walk down the hall first.

Problem, defined

Isolation doesn't need another screen. It needs a neighbor.

One in four adults 65+ is socially isolated, and staff, already stretched thin, rarely have bandwidth to broker connection between residents who'd otherwise never write to each other.

Target users

Two people, two roles.

Who pays for it and who actually uses it aren't the same person, which is worth being clear about.

buys it

The assisted living facility

The customer. Facilities already invest in engagement and companion technology, and this is a purchase decision made at the operations/activities level, not by an individual resident.

uses it

The resident

Someone who wants to connect with a neighbor down the hall, but faces mobility limits, low energy, or simply no natural occasion to reach out first.

Proposed solution

Infrastructure for a relationship, not a replacement for one.

"For nursing home residents with difficulty socializing, we developed a penpal mail carrier robot that delivers notes between residents to facilitate meaningful connection in the community. Our robot acts as infrastructure for interpersonal relationships instead of replacing them, setting us apart from other companion robots because it eliminates the risk of misplaced trust in AI-based relationships."

What's next

Where the route goes from here.

Now

Talk to real users

Interviews and direct observation with assisted-living residents and staff, in both Japan and the US.

Next

New modalities

Different content types, like small gifts or food, and scheduled deliveries tied to seasonal moments.

Later

New cost models

Expanded modalities as cost tiers within facility payments, possibly a non-monetary social currency or gamification system.
