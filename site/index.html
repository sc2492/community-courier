Overview

Some things still mean more handwritten.

Community Message Courier carries notes, drawings, and voice postcards between neighbors in assisted living, so connection doesn't depend on who has the energy to walk down the hall first.

Problem space

Isolation doesn't need another screen. It needs a neighbor.

One in four adults 65+ is socially isolated. Staff, already stretched thin, rarely have bandwidth to facilitate connection between residents who'd otherwise never write to each other.

Target users

Who is this for?

Who pays for it and who actually uses it are different groups.

buys it

Assisted living facilities

Our customers are facilities that already invest in engagement and companion technology, making this a purchase decision made at the operations/activities level.

uses it

Residents

Direct users are residents who want to connect with a neighbor down the hall but face mobility limits, low energy, or simply no natural occasion to reach out first.

Proposed solution

Infrastructure for a relationship, not a replacement for one

"For nursing home residents with difficulty socializing, we developed a penpal mail carrier robot that delivers notes between residents to facilitate meaningful connection in the community. Our robot acts as infrastructure for interpersonal relationships instead of replacing them, setting us apart from other companion robots because it eliminates the risk of misplaced trust in AI-based relationships."
